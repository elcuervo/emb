# Proposal

## Why

EMB already embeds text and exposes real inference, batching, and cache behaviour, but the gallery's small literary atlas cannot show what happens when an actual catalog grows, changes, and needs review. A retail case study using [e-means](https://e-means.github.io/) should demonstrate both the value and the limits of EMB embeddings: reproducible offline centroid training on real products, measured against a strong baseline, followed by honest live query assignment and retrieval.

## What Changes

- **Build a real, versioned retail dataset.** Use two consecutive archived releases of [USDA FoodData Central Branded Foods](https://fdc.nal.usda.gov/download-datasets/), not invented checkout records. Pin the download URLs, digests, extraction rule, source attribution, redistribution check, model files/configuration, and normalization. Profile usable row counts, missing descriptions/categories/GTINs/pack sizes, duplicate or revised products, category imbalance, text lengths, and exclusions before choosing examples or reporting accuracy. Separate product IDs appearing in both releases from genuinely new IDs: USDA says providers can correct descriptions without a product changing. Publish only a bounded, attributable selection and aggregate measurements; do not ship a multi-gigabyte corpus to the gallery.
- **Train e-means offline, with a falsifiable comparison.** Embed the training release through a locally running EMB, using the same named model and preprocessing as live queries. Train frozen centroids with the upstream [Faiss CPU `NumpyEmeans`](https://github.com/facebookresearch/faiss/tree/main/contrib/e_means), not a custom Go clustering implementation. Compare against Faiss Lloyd k-means on the **same vectors, squared-L2 objective, CPU/threads, dataset sizes, centroid counts, and held-out release**, with documented seed and initialization differences, full-data versus subsampling behaviour, passes, peak memory, and end-to-end time. Use normalized input vectors consistently for training and assignment; do not transfer the paper's reported 10x speedup to these data. Reassign all points to the *final* centroids for evaluation: e-means' returned training labels refer to its last epoch, not necessarily the final nearest centroids.
- **Publish a reproducible analysis, not a pretty scatterplot.** Record the dataset/build scripts and machine/model identity alongside generated, inspectable tables and charts: held-out squared-L2 error versus training time/passes (including time to match a specified Lloyd loss, or "not reached"); cluster occupancy and nearly empty centroids; category mix and representative members per cluster; normalized distance and nearest/runner-up margin distributions for held-out and subsequent-release products; and new-item/revised-item slices. Compare multiple data scales, centroid counts, and seeds, reporting variance rather than a lucky run. Keep category labels out of unsupervised training and use them only as an imperfect diagnostic; a cluster is not a product category or a GTIN match. Include real confusing neighbors (e.g. unit versus multipack), failures, and provenance for every displayed number.
- **Add two connected gallery plates, with one clear decision each.** **The catalog match** embeds a fixed example with live `EMB`, searches the existing committed product index for candidates, shows structured identifier/pack evidence and a misleading semantic neighbor, and uses its e-means region only to explain how the catalog is organized; centroids do not prove identity or replace exact retrieval. **The arrival queue** embeds a fixed product from the later release, assigns it to the earlier release's frozen centroids, and compares its distance/margin with held-out earlier products and other arrivals to show when *review*, not automatic classification, is appropriate. Place measured offline quality/cost and release-shift figures next to the live results, separating what EMB did at request time from what e-means and the application did before deployment. A projection is for illustration, never the distance space used for training or assignment.
- **Reuse the current gallery and expose its boundaries.** Extend its single content-hashed SQLite index/manifest with namespaced product samples, frozen centroids, model/release provenance, and generated aggregate evidence; leave Poe/media content intact, including full and partial rebuilds. Use the existing sandbox's allowed `EMB` command, read-only browser-side scoring/search, five-part plate structure, exact-command display, and visible unavailable state. Ship only committed sample inputs, not new visitor free-text or mutable state. Attribute the dataset, e-means/Faiss, and the paper; distinguish a **real product catalog** from any **synthetic POS arrival/load replay**. No claim that these are observed transactions or that centroid training accelerates checkout.

### Evidence and acceptance

- The offline run must be repeatable from pinned inputs and produce the shipped figures without hand-entered winners, percentages, cluster names, timing, or distances. Report training and evaluation population counts, leakage/duplicate treatment, released artifact size, host details, seed spread, and model/centroid compatibility; fail visibly if an index or model mismatch makes live assignments invalid.
- On held-out data and later-release arrivals, report error, occupancy, category mix, and novelty/review examples for **both** clustering methods. The chart shows an e-means advantage only where the measured loss-versus-time curve supports one; otherwise publish the negative result and avoid a speed or quality claim. If there is no defensible review rule, show comparative distances but no automatic "novel" verdict.
- Verify catalog retrieval against an exact float32 reference, and check displayed candidate contradictions with identifiers and pack sizes; nearest-centroid assignment is evaluated separately from nearest-item retrieval. Check the pages in a browser against a local sandbox and an unavailable sandbox, plus the gallery's index/build/published-tree checks. The live path never writes visitor inputs to the shared corpus or handles card/customer information.

### Non-goals

No new EMB command, in-server Faiss dependency, online/streaming e-means, hosted vector database, payment authorization/fraud decision, actual POS ingestion, merchant-private dataset, merchant-wide accuracy claim, guaranteed throughput/p99, or additional analytics dashboard. EMB is the inference endpoint; e-means is **offline training**, and catalog/search/review state belongs to the application. A sale must never wait for enrichment or a human review. The default scope is retail because it provides documented product fields and archived releases; a second vertical is not needed to establish the method.

## Capabilities

### New Capabilities

None. The analysis and its plates extend the existing gallery, not EMB's command surface.

### Modified Capabilities

- `embedding-demos`: Require reproducible, attributable real-corpus analysis and explicitly labelled offline e-means-versus-Lloyd evidence; frozen, model-compatible centroid regions and held-out/later-release diagnostics; live candidate retrieval with structured-data caveats and review/abstention; measured values, honest failure states, and a clear separation between inference, offline training, and application decisions. Preserve the current gallery's read-only, single-index, live-reply, and accessible explanation contracts.

## Impact

- **Offline analysis/index:** Extend `website/tools/demos/` and the existing `website/tools/demo_index.py` stage/finalize path rather than introducing a second shipped index; keep full and product-only rebuilds safe for Poe/media stages. A pinned Faiss/NumPy CPU environment is needed for **offline analysis only**; the existing gallery and Go server acquire no clustering runtime dependency. Full-source downloads and embeddings may need substantial disk/time, so cap the published subset, not the disclosed training population.
- **Site/publication:** New `website/demos/` plates, `website/assets/js/demos.js`, gallery listing, `website/tools/pages.json`, published-tree inventory, sitemap stamp, documentation, and focused data/build/interaction checks. Keep the existing design language: real captions, one emphasized signal, and keyboard-readable alternatives to every chart. Avoid changes under `website/repl/`, whose sandbox deploys separately.
- **Compatibility and risk:** No existing EMB client or command changes. Verify each USDA release's provider/redistribution conditions before committing derived product text. The sandbox's `cache_file` can persist original inputs despite existing site copy claiming user text is ephemeral; fixed published queries prevent these new plates from widening that exposure but do not resolve the older copy's discrepancy. No existing throughput benchmark is evidence for unique POS text, and a simulated arrival rate is an illustration, not a measured transaction workload.
