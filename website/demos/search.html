II.The search

A question the corpus cannot match word for word still finds the passages that mean it. This is the retrieval step in every RAG pipeline, with nothing hidden behind a hosted endpoint.

Ask in your own words. You get back the passages whose numbers are closest — even when they share none of your words.

WHAT YOU ARE LOOKING AT

Ask a question the corpus never contains, phrased the way a person would, and you get back passages from Poe that mean it. Nothing matches on keywords: the query becomes a vector, and the nearest passage vectors come back.

Two layers work together. The index is a vector store, one row per passage, built offline and shipped as a file. The rerank is the server's Lua script, re-ordering those candidates with the model itself — fast and approximate first, exact and small second.

WHAT JUST HAPPENED

Your query went to the sandbox once and came back as a vector — the same model, normalization and window that embedded the corpus, which is the only way the distances mean anything.

The nearest passages came from a vec0 table in a SQLite file your browser downloaded: exact k-nearest-neighbour, your CPU, no server. The seven candidates then went to the preloaded rank.lua, which embedded all eight texts and re-ordered the seven by cosine.

The rerank is bounded by the sandbox: the bridge charges one unit of work per text and refuses a request over eight, so it carries at most seven candidates. The plate sends exactly seven.

WHY IT MATTERS

Retrieval is what turns a language model into something that can read your corpus. The pattern never changes: embed the query with the model that embedded the documents, find the nearest, put them in the prompt.

It is also honest about its arithmetic — int8 weights, int8 index vectors, a measured recall stated rather than implied.

THE EXACT COMMANDS

Three commands, in the order the plate ran them: embed the query, search the shipped file in your browser, rerank on the server.
