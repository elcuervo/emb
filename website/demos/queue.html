IX.The arrival queue

A product from the later catalog release, embedded live and assigned to the earlier release's frozen centroids. The plate shows the distance, the margin, and where both sit against a held-out reference. It also shows why the honest output is often review, which carries no label.

An arrival is a point in the same space. The queue asks which region is nearest, how much nearer it is than the runner-up, and whether the distance falls outside the held-out reference.

THE QUEUE · — fixed arrivals · — · — frozen centroids · held-out p95 — · review, not classification

WHAT YOU ARE LOOKING AT

The centroids were trained on the earlier release, then frozen. An arrival is a row from the later release, embedded by the same model, normalized the same way, and assigned to those frozen centroids.

The earlier release also yields a held-out population the centroids never trained on. Its nearest distances and margins are the reference. An arrival outside that reference can be sent to review. A review flag is not a category or a rejection, and nothing here writes a decision back to a catalog.

For regions inside one corpus, see the atlas. Product identity and quantization are on the catalog plate. This plate covers the later-release reference and abstention.

WHAT JUST HAPPENED

The arrival's text went through the sandbox once. The returned vector was checked against the centroid provenance (model, dimension, L2 normalization), then measured against every frozen centroid in this page with squared L2. The nearest centroid, the runner-up, and the margin between them are computed here from the vectors; the page reads no stored ranking.

The reference numbers come from the generated report: 1 500 held-out products from the training release, assigned to the same frozen centroids. The threshold is the held-out p95 of the nearest distance, written into the report by the analysis. The histogram, the ECDF and the scatter place the live arrival against that population.

The field uses the build-time PCA shipped in the index, so the arrival lands in the held-out cloud's own axes and nothing is fitted in the browser.

WHY IT MATTERS

The later release arrives after the centroids were frozen. This plate measures only rows with no earlier-release history, so every distance here is out of sample.

inside the reference

The distance is within the held-out population. The plate states the comparison and assigns nothing. A nearby region is context only.

outside the reference

The measured distance exceeds the held-out p95. The output is review, and a person looks at the row and its label.

small margin

The runner-up is almost as close. Even inside the reference, a small margin is weak evidence for which region the product belongs to. The plate shows the number and leaves the choice open.

out of sample

The row comes from the later release and has no earlier-release history. The centroids were frozen before it arrived, so its distance is measured on a product they never saw.

THE MEASURED DISTRIBUTION

Held-out and later-release populations on the same frozen centroids. The quantiles are generated from the report, and the live histogram above places one arrival inside them. Every distance is measured from a pinned release, so no synthetic arrival or load value appears in the plots.

THE EXACT COMMANDS

One sandbox call embeds the committed arrival. The centroid table is read and the distances are computed in this page. No command classifies the arrival, and none writes it anywhere.
