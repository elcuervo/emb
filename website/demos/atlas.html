II.The atlas

The corpus laid out by meaning. Every mark is a passage; nearness is similarity. The regions are not decoration — they are where the passages actually cluster, named after what they turned out to be.

FIG. 4 — THE ATLAS · — passages · — · — dimensions · — regions · — at build time · no runtime projection

WHAT YOU ARE LOOKING AT

A vector has a place, and places have neighbours. Draw one author's collected work by that rule and the structure you get is thematic: the sea stories sit together, the confessional tales sit together, and the poems sit somewhere else again. Nobody labelled them. The regions are a k-means clustering over the embeddings, hand-named by reading what landed in each: the ship, the grave, madness, detection, grief.

Two things are deliberately absent. There is no projection work at runtime: the coordinates were computed once, offline, and shipped in the index, so the plate draws rather than renders. And there is no fabricated point: your query lands at the centroid of what it actually retrieved, so if retrieval went somewhere odd the plate shows it rather than tidying it away.

WHAT JUST HAPPENED

Your query was embedded by the sandbox, the index returned its nearest passages, and the plate drew them where the build put them. The switch re-places every passage by publication year instead of by meaning, with the vertical axis left alone: the thematic shape falls apart and the passages scatter by date, which is the point. Meaning and chronology are different orderings of the same corpus.

The blend is the same space used as arithmetic. Two passages are embedded, their vectors are added and scaled by a half, and the corpus passages nearest that midpoint come back — a query built out of no words at all. It is the clearest demonstration that these are coordinates rather than labels.

WHY IT MATTERS

This is what a vector store looks like before you put an API in front of it: points, distances, and regions that turn out to mean something. It is also the argument for keeping the corpus small and legible. A million documents make a cloud; eighty-six works by one author make a map, and a map is what a newcomer can read.

THE EXACT COMMANDS

The plate issues the same three commands as the search, plus the blend. Nothing here selects from the projection at runtime beyond reading the committed rows. 

SELECT rowid, x, y FROM projection WHERE model = 'minilm';
SELECT label, cx, cy, radius, count FROM regions WHERE model = 'minilm';

EMB minilm BLOB "the dead returning"        # the query's vector
SELECT rowid FROM vec_minilm WHERE embedding MATCH vec_int8(:q) AND k = 24;

EMB.EVSHA minilm <between.lua sha1> 8 "the sea" "the grave" "c1" … "c6"
# -> the midpoint's nearest corpus passages, ranked
