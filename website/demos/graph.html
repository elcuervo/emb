IV.The graph

Each passage points at its nearest neighbours. The regions of the atlas are the cloud; here they are the edges, computed by a script beside the model.

A script builds the nearest-neighbour graph of the whole corpus beside the model and returns just the edges.

THE GRAPH · 8 nodes · 16 edges · one batch to embed · computed in Lua

WHAT YOU ARE LOOKING AT

A directed graph: an arrow from one passage to another means the first is closer to the second than to most things. No layout pretends to be geography — the ring is just a place to stand, and the arrows are the only claim. Dense clusters show up as mutual arrows; lonely passages point out and are not pointed at.

WHAT JUST HAPPENED

The page embedded the seed, took the corpus's eight nearest passages, and sent those eight texts to graph.lua. The script called emb.embed once for all eight, built the eight-by-eight cosine matrix, and returned each node's two nearest neighbours.

The matrix never crossed the wire. Sixty-four cosines became sixteen edges inside the server's process, over packed float32 buffers, with emb.math.topk doing the ordering host-side. That is the scripting layer's whole argument: move the arithmetic to the data.

WHY IT MATTERS

Nearest-neighbour graphs are how search finds more than the top-k: recommend what is near what you liked, cluster by connected components, spot the outlier nothing points at. All of it is the same cosine, and all of it is cheap when the graph is built where the vectors already are.

THE EXACT COMMANDS

One scripted command over eight texts and a query, plus the vec0 search that chose the eight.
