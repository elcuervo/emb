V.The zone

Every name under dns.emb.is is a query, and the reply is one TXT record per ranked result. The label is the sentence, the record is the answer, and the working is printed twice: the escaped bytes a resolver hands back beside the glyph they stand for. THE ZONE · dns.emb.is · emojiml · — dimensions · — emoji · — s TTL · one record per result

WHAT YOU ARE LOOKING AT

Every name strictly beneath dns.emb.is is a query. The name's labels are the words of a sentence — i.lost.my.job.dns.emb.is — and +, -, and * separate the terms a query composes. A glyph is a way of writing its vocabulary entry, so 🦈.dns.emb.is and shark.dns.emb.is are the same query, and 🦈-🐟+🐦 is the algebra the joke started as.

A composition adds and subtracts vectors, which is a real question with a dull answer: an average of two emoji is closest to one of them. A conjunction asks a better one — 🐉*🥟 wants the entry closest to all of its terms at once, so it can answer with a third thing entirely, and the terms themselves are never the answer. The reply carries the working: each result's similarity to every term, beside the joint score they multiply to, so a joke can be told from a stretch.

The reply is one TXT record per ranked result, and the record carries the glyph and nothing else: no score, no vocabulary name. A record is a value a resolver caches for its TTL and answers other people's questions from, so it holds the answer rather than the working. That working — the name, the similarity, the escaped bytes — rides the HTTP surface beside the zone, at https://zone.emb.is/<name>, which exists because every resolver renders non-ASCII TXT data escaped: dig prints \240\159\145\184 where the record carries 👍. This plate shows both, because the escaping is the truth about the wire.

    WHAT JUST HAPPENED

    The plate asked the zone's read route for the name. The zone split the labels into terms, spelled each vocabulary word out to its description — the model never sees a raw glyph, because BERT's vocabulary contains almost none — and composed +/- inside scripts/emoji.lua: one emb.embed for the terms, folded left to right. The composed vector is ranked against the index the zone built at boot by embedding every description once.

    A conjunction takes the other path. It needs each term's own vector, so the zone embeds them one by one and ranks every entry by the product of its similarity to each term, skipping the entries the query named. That is what lets an answer be something neither term wrote — and why the ranking shows the legs: the product is only as good as its weakest factor.

    The shape of the question decides the answer's shape. A name whose one term is more than one word is a sentence, and its first record is the whole ranking read as one pictogram; a glyph, a composition, or a conjunction answers with a single emoji. The DNS record and this page's list are the same ranking: one TXT record per result, each holding one glyph, and the escaped form beside each row is that record's data exactly as a resolver's tooling renders it.

    WHY IT MATTERS

    DNS is the most boring protocol there is, and the model answers on it: no SDK, no client library, no port to open beyond the one a resolver already speaks. The retrieval is real — the answer is the nearest description by meaning, not a lookup table — and it is the same ranking the HTTP routes serve.

    The zone is also a second deployment. It runs its own emb on its own machine, which is why the caption above reports this surface's model and vocabulary size and not the sandbox's. It is the gallery's one plate served from another surface of the product, and it says so.
