VI.The medium

Two ways to find a medium, and they answer different questions. A fingerprint names the exact recording from a four-second excerpt, even a noisy one, by matching the constellation of its spectrogram — computed in your browser. An embedding cannot name it at all; it can only say what kind of thing it is. Then a clip is searched frame by frame by a phrase, which is the embedding's half of that trade.

A Shazam-style fingerprint identifies the exact recording live in the browser; emb's model reads the same excerpt as a picture and can only describe it. Then a typed phrase finds the moment in a clip.

WHAT YOU ARE LOOKING AT

A fingerprint is not an embedding. It is a sparse map of the loudest points in a spectrogram, paired into hundreds of short hashes and stored with the time they occurred. Match a query by looking those hashes up: the recording that shares many of them at one consistent offset is the answer, and the size of that agreement is the evidence. It survives noise, because noise does not move the loud points; it does not survive a different performance, because a different performance has different points.

An embedding is the opposite trade. The sandbox's model turns the same excerpt into a picture and a vector, which puts it near other things that look alike — the same instrument, the same texture — but cannot tell one performance from another. Both readings are below, on the same four seconds. Then the video half does with a phrase what the fingerprint does with a hash.

WHAT JUST HAPPENED

The fingerprint ran entirely in your browser. The excerpt was decoded to 11 kHz mono, cut into frames, windowed and transformed with a radix-2 FFT; the loudest local maxima became the constellation; neighbouring peaks were paired into (f1, f2, Δt) hashes. Each hash was looked up in a database built offline from the shipped tracks, and the offset of every hit was counted; the track with hundreds of hits at a single offset won. That histogram is the figure.

The same excerpt was then drawn as a picture and sent to the sandbox's own preloaded zeroshot.lua, which embedded it beside eight candidate descriptions and returned the nearest. It is a different question with a different kind of answer: the first names the recording, the second describes it, and neither is the other. The video half sent one phrase to clip_text.lua for a live text embedding and ranked the sixteen shipped frame vectors the same way the atlas ranks passages.

WHY IT MATTERS

Reach for the cheap, exact tool when the question is exact. A fingerprint costs a few kilobytes per track and answers "which recording is this" better than any model — but it can only ever answer that, and only for a recording it was given. Reach for the embedding when the question is about meaning — "find the moment someone is in the water", "more like this" — and accept that it describes rather than identifies.

What this does not prove. The model read a picture of the audio, not the audio: a spectrogram is a rendering, and the model has no reason to have learned what one means, so its description is a weak guess and the margin is printed to say so. The fingerprint matched the exact recordings in this library and nothing else; a different performance of the same piece would not match, which is the point of it. And the frame search ranks sixteen stills of motion — order and sound are gone before the model sees anything.

THE EXACT COMMANDS

The fingerprint is browser code and no command crosses the wire; the two modelled readings are scripted calls against preloaded presets.
