VI.The medium

Three ways to find a medium, each answering a different question. A fingerprint names the exact recording from a four-second excerpt, even a noisy one, by matching the constellation of its spectrogram — computed in your browser. CLAP, a real text-audio model, cannot name it but finds music by meaning from a phrase. X-CLIP, a real video model, does the same for footage, mixing eight frames inside its graph rather than reading one picture.

A fingerprint names the exact recording; CLAP finds music by meaning and X-CLIP finds footage by meaning — real audio and video models, queried live.

WHAT YOU ARE LOOKING AT

A fingerprint is not an embedding. It is a sparse map of the loudest points in a spectrogram, paired into hundreds of short hashes and stored with the time they occurred. Match a query by looking those hashes up: the recording that shares many of them at one consistent offset is the answer, and the size of that agreement is the evidence. It survives noise, because noise does not move the loud points; it does not survive a different performance, because a different performance has different points.

An embedding is the opposite trade. The sandbox's model turns the same excerpt into a picture and a vector, which puts it near other things that look alike — the same instrument, the same texture — but cannot tell one performance from another. Both readings are below, on the same four seconds. Then the video half does with a phrase what the fingerprint does with a hash.

WHAT JUST HAPPENED

The fingerprint ran entirely in your browser: an 11 kHz decode, a windowed FFT, the loudest local maxima as a constellation, neighbouring peaks paired into (f1, f2, Δt) hashes, each looked up in a database built from the shipped audio, and the offset of every hit counted. The track with hundreds of hits at one offset won. That histogram is the figure.

Then two real models answered the semantic question. CLAP is a text-audio model: the five excerpts were decoded to 48 kHz, turned into the log-mel the model's own feature extractor defines, and embedded by CLAP's audio tower offline; your phrase went to its text tower live and the two were compared in one 512-d space. X-CLIP is a text-video model: each clip was sampled into eight frames and embedded by its video tower, which mixes the frames inside the graph, and your phrase by its text tower. Both phrase embeds are one scripted call each.

WHY IT MATTERS

Reach for the cheap, exact tool when the question is exact. A fingerprint costs a few kilobytes per track and answers "which recording is this" better than any model — but it can only ever answer that, and only for a recording it was given. Reach for the embedding when the question is about meaning — "find the moment someone is in the water", "more like this" — and accept that it describes rather than identifies.

What this does not prove. The fingerprint matched these exact recordings and nothing else — a different performance of the same piece would not match, which is the point of it. CLAP is trained on general audio, so its reading of a genre is a similarity, not a fact: it puts every "guitar" phrase on the lap-steel track and finds the folk guitar only by character. X-CLIP's export is a third party's, and eight evenly-spaced frames is its fixed contract — it mixes motion inside the graph, but the sampling is not a tuning knob, and its margins on this satellite footage are small (0.254 for the launch, 0.216 for the turtle). A close cosine is a retrieval score, not an understanding of the medium.

THE EXACT COMMANDS

The fingerprint is browser code and no command crosses the wire; the two modelled readings are scripted calls against preloaded presets.
