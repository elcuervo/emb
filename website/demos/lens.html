III.The model lens

Two models, one corpus, one query. The neighbours change and the map changes shape, which is why “which embedding?” is a decision and not a detail.

Two models make two different number-worlds. A vector from one is meaningless in the other, so you cannot mix them.

THE LENS · — vs — · — dimensions · one corpus, two tables · incomparable by construction

WHAT YOU ARE LOOKING AT

A model defines a space. Two models define two spaces, and a vector from one means nothing in the other — same numbers, same dimensions, no relationship. This is the fact every newcomer gets wrong, and why a server that serves several models beats one that serves a favourite.

Both models here return 384 dimensions. That is the trap: matching shapes make the mistake easy and silent. Embed the corpus with one, query with the other, and you get plausible, meaningless neighbours. The plate shows the two spaces side by side rather than asserting the difference.

WHAT JUST HAPPENED

The query was embedded once per model and searched against a table per model. Same corpus, same query, two rankings. Where they overlap both models see something; where they do not, each follows its own notion of similarity.

The projection is per model: the build computed one for each, and the switch re-places the passages where that model puts them. A passage at the centre of a region under one model can sit at the edge under another, and neither placement is the real one.

WHY IT MATTERS

Retrieval-tuned, multilingual, small, long-context: each model is a different answer to the same question, and the right one depends on your corpus, language, latency and memory. Keeping the choice inside one server makes the model a config value, not a migration.

The rule is on the plate's own caption: a corpus and its queries must use the same model and normalization. Mixing them does not error, which is what makes it dangerous — it returns a ranking, and the ranking is noise.

THE EXACT COMMANDS

One command per model for the query, one query per table. The index is a single file with a table per model, so switching the lens is choosing a table.
