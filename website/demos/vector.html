I.The vector

Text becomes a fixed-length list of numbers, and that list is the model's answer — not a hash, and not a rendering of the text.

WHAT YOU ARE LOOKING AT

A model turns text into a fixed-length list of numbers. That list is the embedding: the model's answer to what does this text mean, as a point. It is not a hash — the same meaning written two ways lands in nearly the same place, which is why the rest of this gallery works.

Put a passage below and watch what comes back: a waveform of the 384 values, the dimension and byte count from the server, and the first bytes of the reply.

WHAT JUST HAPPENED

Four steps inside the server: tokenize the text into subword units, encode each with its neighbours and position, pool those into one vector, and normalize it to unit length. Pooling makes a sentence one point; normalization is why cosine similarity and the dot product agree.

Truncation is the step's own limit: the model reads a bounded number of tokens, so a longer passage loses its tail silently. The corpus was embedded with the same window and normalization, because query and corpus have to go through the same function or the distances mean nothing.

WHY IT MATTERS

Every later plate is this vector plus arithmetic. If the vector is opaque, everything above it is magic — which is why this is plate one. At 1 536 bytes it is not a sentence either, so a hundred thousand passages fit in tens of megabytes.

THE EXACT COMMANDS

Two commands, shown as the plate issued them. One asks for the packed bytes; the other calls the sandbox's preloaded script, which pools, normalizes and reports the dimension and norm.
