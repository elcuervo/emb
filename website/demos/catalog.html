VIII.The catalog match

A real branded-food catalog, embedded live in the browser: the text becomes a vector, the search returns its nearest products, and telling whether two of them are the same item takes an identifier. Every number below is computed on this page from committed data.

Pick a product to see where it lands in the catalog, what sits near it, and why a close vector does not settle whether two rows are the same item.

THE CATALOG · — products indexed · — · — dimensions · — frozen regions · retrieval — at — · search in this page

WHAT YOU ARE LOOKING AT

The page covers a bounded selection from two consecutive archived releases of the USDA Global Branded Food Products Database, embedded by the same model the sandbox serves. The embedding text is the product's own description and brand. Category is metadata and stays out of the clustering.

A product is a name and a record. EMB sees only the name, which is enough to find products that mean the same thing. The record (GTIN, brand, pack) decides whether two rows are the same item. The two kinds of evidence are kept apart here on purpose.

The map places the live query on the same two build-time axes as the shipped products, so the page shows where it sits; the axes do not preserve distance. This page generates candidates. The retrieval lesson is in search.html, and the vector primitive is in vector.html.

    WHAT JUST HAPPENED

    The sample text went to the sandbox once and came back as 384 float32 values. The page made a unit-length copy for the region and the map, quantized an int8 copy for the index, and asked the committed vec0 table for its nearest rows with that copy. The distances beside the rows are recomputed on this page from the stored float32 vectors, so they can be compared with the offline report.

    The region is a separate calculation. The page asks the shipped centroids for the nearest one and the runner-up, with the margin between them. A region describes how the catalog is organised. It is not a product identity and it does not override the GTIN.

    The projection is build-time as well. PCA was fitted on the shipped product embeddings, and its mean and basis are in the index, so the live query lands on the same two axes as the catalog. Nothing is fitted in the browser.

    WHAT YOU CAN DO WITH IT

    The page is a candidate generator with an explicit evidence check. It does not merge, reject, or publish anything. The three scenarios below are the identity outcomes a downstream system has to handle, and the tray records the decision you would send for a selected candidate.

    same GTIN

    The nearest row carries the same identifier. Similarity and evidence agree, so this is a merge candidate, still subject to pack and brand checks.

    different GTIN, close

    The names are close and the identifiers differ, so the row goes to review: a different pack, a private label, or a product that really is different. The page shows the contradiction and does not resolve it.

    revised row

    USDA lets a provider correct a description while the product stays the same. The report counts that as a revision and keeps the ID.

    THE MEASURED COMPARISON

    The two centroid methods run on the same matrix and are scored the same way: held-out squared L2 after every held-out product is assigned to the final centroids. The plot and the table are generated from the report, and no value on them is typed into this page.

    THE EXACT COMMANDS

    The first command goes to the sandbox and embeds the sample by name. Both SELECTs run in this page: one searches the shipped table, the other reads the frozen centroids.
